EXAM-SESSION: CA1-NEURO-FALL24PAGE 1 OF 1

Long-term potentiation in the CA1 hippocampal subfield relies on the synchronized activity of both ionotropic glutamate receptors. Under basal conditions, glutamate binding to AMPA receptors promotes rapid sodium (Na+) influx, initiating a transient excitatory postsynaptic potential (EPSP).

However, NMDA receptors remain largely inactive at resting potentials because hydrated magnesium ions (Mg2+) physically obstruct the channel pore. When high-frequency tetanus triggers sustained AMPA receptor activation, the resultant substantial depolarization expels the Mg2+ block, allowing a large influx of calcium ions (Ca2+) into the dendritic spine head.

This elevated calcium acts as a second messenger that recruits calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII). Concurrently, postsynaptic stimulation activates nitric oxide synthase (NOS), prompting the retrograde diffusion of nitric oxide (NO) back across the synaptic cleft to enhance presynaptic vesicle exocytosis probability during subsequent potentials.
